# Verbs and the Simple Tenses

A **verb** shows an action (*run*, *write*, *cook*) or a state of being (*is*, *seem*, *become*). Every complete sentence has at least one verb.

## Action verbs, linking verbs and helping verbs

- **Action verbs** tell what the subject does: *Lito plays basketball.*
- **Linking verbs** connect the subject to a word that describes or renames it: *The soup is hot. Maria became class president.* Common linking verbs: *am, is, are, was, were, seem, become, look, feel, taste*.
- **Helping (auxiliary) verbs** come before the main verb: *She is studying. They have finished. We will go.*

## The three simple tenses

**Simple present** is for habits, routines, facts and things that are always true.
- Form: base verb, or base verb + -s/-es with *he, she, it* or one person or thing.
- *I walk to school every day. She walks to school. The sun rises in the east. Water boils at 100 °C.*

**Simple past** is for actions that started and finished in the past. Time words: *yesterday, last week, ago, in 2020*.
- Regular verbs add -ed: *walk → walked*, *study → studied*, *stop → stopped*.
- Irregular verbs change form: *go → went*, *eat → ate*, *see → saw*, *buy → bought*, *write → wrote*.
- *We visited Baguio last summer.*

**Simple future** is for actions that will happen later. Time words: *tomorrow, next week, later, soon*.
- Form: *will* + base verb, or *am/is/are going to* + base verb.
- *I will call you tomorrow. They are going to plant trees on Saturday.*

## Questions and negatives

Use *do/does* (present) or *did* (past) with the base verb:
- *Do you like mangoes? She does not (doesn't) eat meat. Did they win? He didn't go.*

A common mistake is keeping the past form after *did*: say *Did you see it?* not *Did you saw it?* Only one verb in the group shows the tense.
