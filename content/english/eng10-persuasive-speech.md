# Persuasive Speeches and Rhetorical Devices

A **persuasive speech** tries to make listeners believe something or take action. It uses clear reasons, evidence and the speaker's voice and delivery.

## Ethos, pathos and logos

The Greek philosopher Aristotle described three ways to persuade:
- **Ethos (credibility)**: the audience trusts the speaker. Show that you are knowledgeable, fair and honest — mention your experience, cite reliable sources, and respect other views. *As a member of the school's environment club for three years…*
- **Pathos (emotion)**: appeal to the audience's feelings — hope, pride, fear, sympathy — through stories and vivid images. *Imagine your little brother wading through floodwater full of plastic.*
- **Logos (logic)**: use facts, statistics, examples and clear reasoning. *Our school uses about 2,000 plastic cups a week.*

The strongest speeches balance all three. Emotion without facts can feel manipulative; facts without emotion can feel dull.

## Rhetorical devices

- **Rhetorical question**: a question asked for effect, not for an answer. *How many more floods must we suffer?*
- **Repetition / anaphora**: repeating a word or phrase at the start of sentences. *We will plant. We will clean. We will not give up.*
- **Rule of three (tricolon)**: listing ideas in threes because it sounds complete. *Reduce, reuse, recycle.*
- **Parallelism**: similar grammatical structure for balance.
- **Antithesis**: contrasting ideas side by side. *Ask not what your country can do for you — ask what you can do for your country.*
- **Direct address**: speaking to the audience as *you* and including them as *we*.
- **Anecdote**: a short personal story that makes a point memorable.

## Structure and delivery

1. **Opening**: grab attention with a story, question, quote or striking fact, then state your position.
2. **Body**: give two or three reasons with evidence; answer an opposing view.
3. **Closing**: summarize and end with a clear **call to action** — what you want the audience to do.

Delivery matters: speak clearly and not too fast, vary your volume and pace, pause before key points, make eye contact, stand confidently and use natural gestures. Practice aloud several times, and time yourself.
