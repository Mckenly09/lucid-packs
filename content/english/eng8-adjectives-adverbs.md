# Adjectives, Adverbs and Degrees of Comparison

**Adjectives** describe nouns and pronouns. They answer *what kind? which one? how many?* — *a **tall** tree, **this** jeepney, **three** cats*.

**Adverbs** describe verbs, adjectives or other adverbs. They answer *how? when? where? how often? to what degree?* — *She runs **quickly**. We left **early**. It is **very** hot. He **always** smiles.* Many adverbs end in -ly (*slowly, carefully*), but not all (*fast, well, soon, often, here*).

## Adjective or adverb?

Use an adjective after a linking verb (*is, seem, feel, look, taste*) and an adverb after an action verb:
- *The food tastes **good**.* (adjective: describes *food*)
- *She cooks **well**.* (adverb: describes *cooks*)
- *I feel **bad** about it* (not *badly*). *He sang **badly**.*

## Order of adjectives

When several adjectives come before a noun, English usually follows this order: **opinion, size, age, shape, color, origin, material, purpose**, then the noun.
- *a **beautiful small old** wooden house*
- *a **big red** Filipino flag*

## Degrees of comparison

- **Positive** describes one thing: *Mt. Apo is **high**.*
- **Comparative** compares two things, usually with *than*: *Mt. Apo is **higher than** Mt. Pulag.*
- **Superlative** compares three or more, usually with *the*: *Mt. Apo is **the highest** mountain in the Philippines.*

How to form them:
- One syllable: add -er / -est. *tall, taller, tallest; big, bigger, biggest.*
- Two syllables ending in -y: change *y* to *i* and add -er / -est. *happy, happier, happiest.*
- Most words with two or more syllables: use *more / most* (or *less / least*). *beautiful, more beautiful, most beautiful.*
- Irregular: *good, better, best; bad, worse, worst; many/much, more, most; little, less, least; far, farther, farthest.*

Adverbs compare the same way: *fast, faster, fastest; carefully, more carefully, most carefully; well, better, best.*

Don't double up: say *better* (not *more better*) and *the easiest* (not *the most easiest*).
