# Writing a Paragraph

A **paragraph** is a group of sentences about one main idea. A good paragraph has three parts: a topic sentence, supporting sentences and a concluding sentence.

## The three parts

- **Topic sentence**: states the main idea and the writer's point about it. It is usually first. *Joining a school club helps students in many ways.*
- **Supporting sentences**: give reasons, facts, examples or details that explain the topic sentence. Most paragraphs have three to five. *First, clubs teach teamwork…*
- **Concluding sentence**: wraps up the paragraph by restating the main idea in new words or giving a final thought. *For these reasons, every student should try at least one club.*

## Unity and coherence

- **Unity**: every sentence is about the main idea. Remove any sentence that wanders off-topic.
- **Coherence**: the sentences are in a logical order and connected, so the reader follows easily. Use transition words (*first, also, for example, however, as a result, finally*) and keep the same point of view and tense.

Common ways to organize a paragraph:
- **Chronological** (time order): for stories and steps in a process.
- **Spatial**: for describing a place, from left to right or near to far.
- **Order of importance**: from least to most important reason, or the reverse.
- **Compare and contrast**: similarities and differences.
- **Cause and effect**: why something happens and what results.

## Example

*Joining a school club helps students in many ways. First, clubs teach teamwork because members plan events and solve problems together. In addition, students discover new interests, such as journalism, robotics or theater, that may lead to future careers. Clubs also build confidence, since members practice speaking and leading in front of others. For these reasons, every student should try at least one club.*

## Checklist

1. Does my topic sentence state one clear main idea?
2. Does every sentence support it?
3. Did I use examples or facts, not just general statements?
4. Are my ideas in a clear order, with transitions?
5. Does my last sentence close the paragraph?
6. Did I check spelling, capitalization, punctuation and subject-verb agreement?
