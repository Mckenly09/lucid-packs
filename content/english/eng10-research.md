# Research: Sources, Paraphrasing and Citing

## Kinds of sources

- **Primary sources** are first-hand: original documents, interviews, surveys you conduct, diaries, photographs, data from an experiment, a speech recording. *Rizal's letters.*
- **Secondary sources** explain or analyze primary sources: textbooks, encyclopedia articles, documentaries, reviews, news analysis. *A biography of Rizal.*

Judge a source with **CRAAP**: **Currency** (is it recent enough?), **Relevance** (does it answer your question?), **Authority** (who wrote it and are they qualified?), **Accuracy** (is it supported by evidence and confirmed elsewhere?), **Purpose** (to inform, sell or persuade?). Websites ending in .gov, .edu and established organizations are usually more reliable than anonymous posts.

## Taking notes

Record the source details as you go: author, title, date, publisher or website, and page or URL. Write notes in your own words, and mark any exact words with quotation marks so you know they are quotes.

## Quoting, paraphrasing and summarizing

- **Quoting**: copy the exact words in quotation marks. Use it when the wording itself matters. Keep quotes short.
- **Paraphrasing**: restate one idea fully in your own words and sentence structure, about the same length. Changing only a few words is not paraphrasing.
- **Summarizing**: give only the main ideas of a longer text, much shorter.

To paraphrase well: read the passage until you understand it, close it, write the idea from memory, then compare with the original to check accuracy and that you didn't copy phrases.

All three still need a citation. Using someone's words or ideas without credit is **plagiarism**, even if it is accidental.

## Citing sources

A citation tells the reader where an idea came from. Schools often use **APA** or **MLA** style.

- **APA in-text**: author's last name and year — *(Santos, 2021)*. With a quote, add the page: *(Santos, 2021, p. 14)*.
- **APA reference list entry** (book): *Santos, M. (2021). Title of the book in italics. Publisher.*
- **MLA in-text**: author's last name and page — *(Santos 14)*.
- **MLA works cited** (book): *Santos, Maria. Title of the Book in Italics. Publisher, 2021.*

Put all sources in a reference list (APA) or works-cited list (MLA) at the end, in alphabetical order by author's last name. Follow the exact style your teacher asks for.
