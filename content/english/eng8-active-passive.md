# Active and Passive Voice

**Voice** tells whether the subject does the action or receives it.

- **Active voice**: the subject does the action. *The students **planted** the trees.*
- **Passive voice**: the subject receives the action. *The trees **were planted** by the students.*

## How to form the passive

Passive = a form of **be** + the **past participle** of the main verb. The doer, if mentioned, comes after *by*.

The tense is carried by *be*:
- Simple present: *Rice **is grown** in Nueva Ecija.*
- Simple past: *The bridge **was built** in 1990.*
- Simple future: *The results **will be announced** tomorrow.*
- Present progressive: *The road **is being repaired**.*
- Present perfect: *The letters **have been sent**.*
- With a modal: *The form **must be signed** by a parent.*

Only verbs that take an object (transitive verbs) can be made passive. *She arrived* has no object, so it has no passive form.

## Changing active to passive

1. Make the object of the active sentence the new subject.
2. Use the correct form of *be* in the same tense, plus the past participle.
3. Put the old subject after *by*, or leave it out.

*Active: Jose Rizal **wrote** Noli Me Tangere.* → *Passive: Noli Me Tangere **was written** by Jose Rizal.*

## When to use each

Use the **active voice** most of the time. It is shorter, clearer and more direct.

Use the **passive voice** when:
- the doer is unknown: *My bike **was stolen**.*
- the doer is obvious or unimportant: *The suspect **was arrested**.*
- you want to focus on the action or result, as in science reports: *The water **was heated** to 80 °C.*

Avoid the passive when it hides who is responsible: *Mistakes were made* says less than *I made a mistake*.
