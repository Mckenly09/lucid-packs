# Kinds and Structures of Sentences

A **sentence** is a group of words with a subject and a verb that expresses a complete thought. *The rain stopped.*

## Four kinds of sentences (by purpose)

- **Declarative** makes a statement and ends with a period: *The jeepney is full.*
- **Interrogative** asks a question and ends with a question mark: *Where is the library?*
- **Imperative** gives a command or request; the subject *you* is understood: *Close the door, please.*
- **Exclamatory** shows strong feeling and ends with an exclamation point: *What a beautiful sunset!*

## Clauses

A **clause** has a subject and a verb.
- An **independent clause** can stand alone as a sentence: *I studied hard.*
- A **dependent clause** cannot stand alone. It starts with a word like *because, although, when, if, since, while, who, which, that*: *because the test was difficult*.

## Four sentence structures

- **Simple**: one independent clause. *Carlo plays the guitar.* (A simple sentence can still have two subjects or two verbs: *Carlo and Joy sing and dance.*)
- **Compound**: two independent clauses joined by a comma and a coordinating conjunction (*for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so* — remember FANBOYS), or by a semicolon. *It rained, so the game was cancelled. I like math; my brother likes art.*
- **Complex**: one independent clause and at least one dependent clause. *Because it rained, the game was cancelled.* When the dependent clause comes first, put a comma after it.
- **Compound-complex**: two or more independent clauses and at least one dependent clause. *When the bell rang, the students left, but the teacher stayed.*

## Why it matters

Using different structures makes writing clearer and less repetitive. Short simple sentences are good for emphasis; complex sentences show how ideas are connected, such as cause and effect (*because*), contrast (*although*) or time (*when*).
