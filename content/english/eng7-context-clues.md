# Context Clues and Word Meaning

**Context clues** are hints in the words and sentences around an unfamiliar word that help you guess its meaning without a dictionary.

## Kinds of context clues

- **Definition**: the sentence explains the word directly, often after *is, means, refers to* or between commas or dashes. *A **typhoon**, a strong tropical storm with heavy rain and wind, hit the province.*
- **Synonym**: a word with a similar meaning appears nearby. *The old house was **dilapidated**, or run-down.*
- **Antonym (contrast)**: a word with the opposite meaning appears, often after *but, however, unlike, although*. *Unlike his **timid** brother, Paolo was bold and talkative.* (timid = shy)
- **Example**: examples show what the word means, after *such as, like, for example, including*. *We saw many **marine** animals, such as dolphins, sea turtles and whale sharks.* (marine = of the sea)
- **Inference (general sense)**: you use the whole situation. *After walking for six hours under the sun, the hikers were **famished** and ate everything.* (famished = very hungry)

## Word parts

Prefixes, roots and suffixes also give clues.
- Prefixes: **un-** / **dis-** / **in-** = not (*unhappy, dishonest, incorrect*); **re-** = again (*rewrite*); **pre-** = before (*preview*); **mis-** = wrongly (*misread*).
- Suffixes: **-less** = without (*careless*); **-ful** = full of (*hopeful*); **-able** = can be done (*readable*); **-er / -or** = a person who (*teacher, actor*).
- Roots: **bio** = life, **geo** = earth, **graph** = write, **port** = carry, **tele** = far.

## Steps when you meet a new word

1. Read the whole sentence and the ones before and after it.
2. Look for a clue word (*or, but, such as, means*).
3. Check the word's parts.
4. Guess a meaning and put it in place of the word. If the sentence still makes sense, your guess is probably right.
5. If it doesn't, check a dictionary.
