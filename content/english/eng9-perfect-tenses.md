# Perfect and Progressive Tenses

Besides the simple tenses, English uses **progressive** tenses (actions in progress) and **perfect** tenses (actions completed before another time). The helping verb shows the time; the main verb is an -ing form or a past participle.

## Progressive tenses: be + verb-ing

- **Present progressive**: happening now or around now. *I **am reading** a novel. They **are practicing** for the contest this week.*
- **Past progressive**: in progress at a moment in the past, often interrupted. *We **were eating** dinner when the lights went out.*
- **Future progressive**: will be in progress at a future time. *At 8 a.m. tomorrow, I **will be taking** the exam.*

Some verbs that describe states are rarely progressive: *know, like, love, want, believe, own, need*. Say *I know the answer* (not *I am knowing*).

## Perfect tenses: have + past participle

The past participle of regular verbs ends in -ed (*finished*). Irregular verbs have their own forms: *go – went – **gone**, eat – ate – **eaten**, write – wrote – **written**, see – saw – **seen**, take – took – **taken***.

- **Present perfect** (*has/have* + participle): an action at an unstated time before now, an experience, or an action that started in the past and continues. *I **have visited** Vigan twice. She **has lived** in Iloilo since 2019. We **have** just **finished**.*
  - Use **for** with a length of time (*for three years*) and **since** with a starting point (*since 2019, since Monday*).
  - Don't use the present perfect with a finished time: *I saw him yesterday* (not *I have seen him yesterday*).
- **Past perfect** (*had* + participle): an action completed before another past action. *When we arrived, the movie **had** already **started**.*
- **Future perfect** (*will have* + participle): an action that will be completed before a future time. *By June, I **will have finished** Grade 9.*

## Perfect progressive tenses

These show an action continuing up to a point:
- *She **has been studying** for two hours.* (still studying now)
- *They **had been waiting** for an hour when the bus came.*
- *By 5 p.m., I **will have been working** for eight hours.*

## Choosing a tense

Ask: Is it finished or still going? Does it come before another event? Is the time stated? A timeline with the events marked often makes the right tense clear.
