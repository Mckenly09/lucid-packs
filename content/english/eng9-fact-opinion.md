# Fact, Opinion and Bias

Good readers and listeners separate what can be proven from what someone believes, and notice when a writer is leaning to one side.

## Fact and opinion

- A **fact** can be checked and proven true or false using records, measurement, observation or reliable sources. *Mayon Volcano is in Albay. The Philippines has more than 7,600 islands. The test had 50 items.*
- An **opinion** expresses a belief, feeling or judgment. People can disagree with it, and it cannot be proven. *Mayon is the most beautiful volcano in the world. Online classes are better than face-to-face classes.*

Clue words for opinions:
- Judgment words: *best, worst, beautiful, boring, important, should, must, ought*.
- Belief phrases: *I think, I believe, in my opinion, it seems, probably*.

Some sentences mix both: *Rizal wrote two novels (fact), and they are the greatest Filipino books ever written (opinion).* An opinion is stronger when it is **supported by facts** — that is an informed opinion.

## Bias

**Bias** is a one-sided view that favors or opposes a person, group or idea, often unfairly. A biased text may still contain true facts but chooses and presents them to push one side.

Signs of bias:
- Only one side of an issue is shown; opposing views are ignored or mocked.
- **Loaded words** that carry strong feelings: *heroic* vs. *reckless*, *protesters* vs. *troublemakers*, *a tax relief* vs. *a tax cut for the rich*.
- Facts are left out, or numbers are shown without context.
- Generalizations about whole groups: *All teenagers are lazy.*
- The writer gains something (money, votes, followers) if you agree.

## Checking a source

Ask:
1. Who wrote it, and what is their purpose — to inform, persuade, sell or entertain?
2. Is the information supported with evidence and sources?
3. Do other reliable sources (government agencies, established news outlets, encyclopedias) say the same thing?
4. Is it recent?
5. Does the headline match the article, or is it made to get clicks?

These habits help you spot fake news and make fair decisions.
