# Conjunctions and Transition Words

**Conjunctions** join words, phrases and clauses. **Transition words** connect sentences and paragraphs and show how ideas relate. Both make writing smooth and logical.

## Coordinating conjunctions

The seven coordinating conjunctions are **FANBOYS**: *for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so*. They join equal parts.
- *Rice **and** fish* (words); *in the morning **or** at night* (phrases).
- To join two complete sentences, put a comma before the conjunction: *I was tired, **but** I finished my homework. It was raining, **so** we stayed inside.*

## Subordinating conjunctions

These begin a dependent clause and show its relationship to the main clause:
- Cause: *because, since, as* — *We stayed home **because** it was raining.*
- Contrast: *although, though, even though, while* — ***Although** he was sick, he went to school.*
- Time: *when, before, after, until, while, as soon as* — *Call me **when** you arrive.*
- Condition: *if, unless, provided that* — *You will pass **if** you study.*

## Correlative conjunctions

These work in pairs: *both … and, either … or, neither … nor, not only … but also, whether … or*.
- ***Not only** did she win, **but** she **also** broke the record.*
- Keep the parts after each word parallel: *either by bus or by jeepney* (not *either by bus or riding a jeepney*).

## Transition words by purpose

- Adding an idea: *also, in addition, furthermore, moreover, besides*
- Contrast: *however, on the other hand, nevertheless, in contrast, instead*
- Cause and effect: *therefore, as a result, consequently, thus, so*
- Example: *for example, for instance, such as, in particular*
- Sequence: *first, next, then, after that, finally*
- Conclusion: *in conclusion, in summary, overall, to sum up*

A transition that joins two independent clauses uses a semicolon before it and a comma after it: *The road was flooded; **therefore**, classes were suspended.* At the start of a sentence, follow it with a comma: ***However**, the rain stopped by noon.*
