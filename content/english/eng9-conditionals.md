# Conditional Sentences

A **conditional sentence** says that one thing happens if another thing happens. It has two clauses: the **if-clause** (the condition) and the **main clause** (the result). Either can come first; when the if-clause comes first, use a comma.

*If it rains, we will stay home. = We will stay home if it rains.*

## The four main types

**Zero conditional — facts and things that are always true**
- Form: *if* + simple present, simple present.
- *If you heat ice, it melts. If plants don't get water, they die.*
- *If* can be replaced by *when*.

**First conditional — real or likely future situations**
- Form: *if* + simple present, *will* + base verb.
- *If you study tonight, you will pass the quiz. If it doesn't stop raining, classes will be suspended.*
- Don't use *will* in the if-clause: *If it rains* (not *If it will rain*).

**Second conditional — unreal or unlikely present or future situations**
- Form: *if* + simple past, *would* + base verb.
- *If I had a million pesos, I would build a library. If she lived closer, she would visit more often.*
- With *be*, formal English uses *were* for all subjects: *If I **were** you, I would apologize.*

**Third conditional — unreal past situations (imagining a different past)**
- Form: *if* + past perfect, *would have* + past participle.
- *If we had left earlier, we would have caught the bus.* (We didn't leave early, so we missed it.)
- *If he had studied, he would have passed.*

## Unless and other words

- **Unless** means *if … not*: *You will be late **unless** you hurry* = *if you don't hurry*.
- *Provided that, as long as, in case* also introduce conditions: *You can go **as long as** you finish your chores.*

## Quick check

Ask whether the situation is a fact (zero), possible (first), imaginary now (second) or imaginary in the past (third), then match the verb forms.
