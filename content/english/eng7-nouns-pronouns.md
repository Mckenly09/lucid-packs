# Nouns and Pronouns

A **noun** names a person, place, thing, animal or idea: *teacher*, *Cebu*, *notebook*, *carabao*, *honesty*.

## Kinds of nouns

- **Common nouns** name any member of a group and start with a small letter: *city*, *river*, *school*.
- **Proper nouns** name one particular person, place or thing and start with a capital letter: *Manila*, *Pasig River*, *Rizal High School*.
- **Concrete nouns** can be seen, heard, touched, tasted or smelled: *rice*, *rain*, *guitar*.
- **Abstract nouns** name ideas, feelings and qualities: *freedom*, *joy*, *courage*.
- **Collective nouns** name a group as one unit: *a class of students*, *a flock of birds*, *a team of players*.
- **Count nouns** can be counted and have a plural (*one book, two books*). **Mass nouns** cannot be counted and usually have no plural (*water*, *sugar*, *advice*). Say *much water* but *many books*.

Most plurals add -s or -es (*bag → bags*, *box → boxes*). Some are irregular: *child → children*, *man → men*, *tooth → teeth*, *mouse → mice*.

## Pronouns

A **pronoun** takes the place of a noun so we don't repeat it: *Ana lost her pen, so she borrowed mine.* The noun a pronoun replaces is its **antecedent** (here, *Ana*).

- **Subject pronouns** do the action: *I, you, he, she, it, we, they*. *She sings.*
- **Object pronouns** receive the action or follow a preposition: *me, you, him, her, it, us, them*. *The teacher called him. Sit beside me.*
- **Possessive pronouns** show ownership: *mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs*. *The red bag is mine.*
- **Reflexive pronouns** end in -self or -selves and point back to the subject: *Marco hurt himself.*

## Common mistakes

- Use the subject form before a verb: *Ben and I went home* (not *Ben and me went*). Test it by removing the other person: *I went home*.
- Use the object form after a preposition: *between you and me* (not *between you and I*).
- A pronoun must agree with its antecedent in number: *Each student brought his or her book*, or *The students brought their books*.
- English pronouns show gender (*he*, *she*), unlike Filipino *siya*. Choose *he* or *she* to match the person.
