# Main Idea, Supporting Details and Summarizing

## Main idea

The **main idea** is the most important point a paragraph or text makes — what the writer wants you to understand. The **topic** is what the text is about in one or two words (*plastic waste*); the main idea is what the writer says about it (*Plastic waste is harming Philippine coral reefs*).

The main idea is often stated in a **topic sentence**, usually the first sentence of a paragraph, sometimes the last. If it is not stated, it is **implied**, and you put it into your own words.

To find it, ask:
1. What is the topic?
2. What does the writer say about the topic again and again?
3. Which sentence could be the "umbrella" that all the other sentences fit under?

## Supporting details

**Supporting details** explain, prove or describe the main idea. They can be facts, examples, statistics, reasons, steps, quotes or descriptions.
- **Major details** directly support the main idea.
- **Minor details** give more information about the major details.

If you remove a major detail, the main idea becomes weaker. If a sentence doesn't support the main idea at all, it is off-topic.

## Summarizing

A **summary** is a short retelling of a text in your own words that keeps only the main idea and the major details.

Steps:
1. Read the whole text and identify the main idea.
2. List the major supporting details.
3. Leave out examples, minor details, repeated ideas and your own opinion.
4. Write it in your own words, much shorter than the original — often one paragraph or one-third of the length or less.
5. Check that someone who hasn't read the text would understand its main point.

For a story, a summary answers **who, what, when, where, why and how**, or follows *somebody – wanted – but – so – then*: *Juan wanted to join the contest, but he had no shoes, so he borrowed his cousin's, then he won second place.*

**Paraphrasing** restates one passage in your own words at about the same length; **summarizing** shortens the whole text.
